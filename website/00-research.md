# Website Research: What the Best Branches and Clubs Actually Do

Field notes from studying the strongest 180DC branch sites, elite non-180 student consulting clubs, and the Dutch competitive landscape — plus an honest read of our own current page. Feeds [01-brainstorm.md](01-brainstorm.md). Researched 2026-07-08.

## Our starting point: the current branch page

[180dc.org/branches/Delft-Rotterdam](https://180dc.org/branches/Delft-Rotterdam) — the global-site template page:

- **What works:** the positioning line is genuinely distinctive — *first multi-city branch, TU Delft technical + Erasmus Rotterdam business*. Leadership team listed with photos and emails. Service areas named.
- **What's broken:** "Latest Case Studies — *Coming soon*." "Partners — *No items found*." Zero evidence of work done. No project outcomes, no client voices, no impact numbers. The two sections a skeptical non-profit director or sponsor would check first are literally empty.
- **Conclusion:** the current page tells people we exist; it gives nobody a reason to choose us. That gap defines the website's job.

## Exemplar 1 — 180DC UCLA ([ucla180dc.org](https://www.ucla180dc.org/)) — Best Overall Branch, Americas, 3 years running

- Nav: Home, Services, Partnerships, Team (Members + Alumni), Impact, Join, Case Competition, Contact.
- **The dual-logo strategy (their masterstroke):** two separate logo walls — clients served (Habitat for Humanity, Red Cross, Teach for America…) *and* member career destinations (McKinsey, BCG, Google…). One page simultaneously answers the client's question ("are they credible?") and the student's question ("what does this do for my career?").
- Awards front and center — they lead with "Best Overall Branch 3× " before client count.
- Explicit quantification: "80+ projects completed."
- Three-pillar content model (Projects / People / Partners) — each stakeholder type finds their door.
- Runs a **case competition** as a page-worthy flagship event (recruitment + partnerships + brand in one).

## Exemplar 2 — 180DC Berkeley ([180dcberkeley.com](https://www.180dcberkeley.com/))

- Slimmer: About, Services, People, Initiatives, Apply Now.
- Leans on global-network stats (10,000+ volunteers, 170+ branches) where local proof is thin — a useful crutch for a young branch, and a warning: global stats are the *fallback*, not the goal.
- Presidents' welcome message with names and term — cheap authenticity, humanizes a club with full turnover.
- Weakness worth noting: engagement areas named but no concrete case studies on the homepage — same gap we have.

## Exemplar 3 — 180DC Leuven ([180dcleuven.org](https://www.180dcleuven.org/)) — closest peer (European, founded 2019, like us)

- Nav splits **Student@180DC** (Application Info, FAQ, Impact Report, Testimonies & Alumni) from **Projects** (Current, Past) — clean audience separation.
- **The 8-week visual project timeline** with photos and named client touchpoints (weeks 2 and 5) — makes the abstract "we do a consulting project" concrete and unscary for a first-time non-profit client. Best single explanatory device found in the whole survey.
- Publishes an **Impact Report** — an annual artifact that compounds credibility year over year.
- FAQ page for applicants — cuts recruitment-season email load.

## Exemplar 4 — Berkeley Consulting ([bc.studentorg.berkeley.edu](https://bc.studentorg.berkeley.edu/)) — elite non-180 club, founded 1996

- Nav: Home, Services, Members, Alumni, Join, FAQ, Contact.
- **Alumni as a first-class page** — 30 years of placement tracking is their recruitment engine. Career outcomes are the product they sell to applicants.
- Client logo carousel (Airbnb, Samsung…) linked to case studies — logos are the hook, cases are the substance.
- Dual CTAs everywhere: "Work with us" / "Join our team" — the two conversion paths never compete for one button.
- Playful humanity amid professionalism (their stats row: "Years, Projects, Meals").

## Exemplar 5 — Academy Consult München ([academyconsult.de](https://academyconsult.de/)) — German student consultancy, 550+ projects

- **Dual-audience architecture taken furthest:** literally two site pathways — *Unternehmen* (companies) and *Studierende* (students) — with separate nav, separate content, separate application portal (bewerben.academyconsult.de).
- **Transparent 5-stage engagement process** (contact → staffing → proposal → execution → closeout) — like Leuven's timeline, it de-risks the purchase for the client.
- Client testimonials from names that matter (Siemens, Audi, TUM professor: students "think more radically") — endorsement quotes beat self-description.
- **Two-tier partner presentation:** partner firms (Bain, Deloitte, PwC — the sponsor/recruiter tier) vs competence partners (BCG, SystemIQ — expertise tier). Partners are segmented by what they give, and each tier knows what it's buying.
- Value positioning stated out loud: comparable results at a fraction of consulting cost, "reliable partner with student lightness" — youth framed as an asset, never apologized for.

## Context — uniPartners ([unipartners.nl](https://www.unipartners.nl/)): the Dutch incumbent

- "Largest and oldest student consultancy in NL" (since 1987), 11 university cities, paid projects, 30+ client logos, professional board structure per city including a dedicated Quality Manager.
- **What this means for us:** in the Dutch market, "students doing consulting" is a known, commercialized category. Our differentiation cannot be "students + consulting" — it must be (a) **social impact / pro bono** (they serve SMEs for fees; we serve non-profits free), (b) **the global 180DC brand**, and (c) **our AI-augmented quality system**, which literally no student consultancy anywhere can show.

## The patterns (what everything above reduces to)

1. **Two audiences, two doors.** Every strong site separates the client path from the student path — best-in-class (Academy Consult) makes them parallel universes. Weak sites make everyone read the same About page. We have *three* doors: clients, students, partners/sponsors.
2. **Proof beats prose.** Logo walls, project counts, awards, testimonials, impact reports. Nobody strong leads with mission statements; they lead with evidence. Our current page has zero evidence in it.
3. **Show the machine.** Leuven's 8-week timeline and Academy Consult's 5-stage process convert nervous first-time clients by making the engagement predictable. We should show ours — and ours genuinely has more to show (scoping memo, quality reviewer, murder board).
4. **Careers are the recruitment product.** UCLA's destination logos, Berkeley Consulting's alumni page. Students join for what they leave with. Our Skill Passport idea is this, productized beyond what any of them do.
5. **The site compounds or it rots.** Impact reports, case studies, alumni pages grow more valuable yearly — but only with an operating model behind them (uniPartners has a Quality Manager; we have full turnover every 3 years). A student-org website is a content pipeline problem wearing a design costume.
6. **Nobody has our story.** No branch or club surveyed shows *how* they assure quality beyond claiming it. An open, documented AI quality system — with a public repo, published methodology, and honest eval discipline — is a differentiator no logo wall can match, and it doubles as the partner-tier pitch.
