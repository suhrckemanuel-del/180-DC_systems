Week 0 — scoping call. A structured discovery conversation: your challenge, your constraints, what success looks like. One hour, no commitment.

Week 1 — proposal. A written scope you sign off on: what we will answer, what we won't, what you'll hold at the end.

Weeks 2–11 — the project. A team of 4–6 student consultants with a dedicated lead; a mid-point review where you course-correct.

Final week. A board-ready presentation, delivered to you and yours, every underlying analysis handed over.

What the signed proposal fixes

  • The one question we will answer, in your words
  • The evidence we will gather to answer it
  • The deliverable you hold at the end, and its format
  • The two dates you are in the room: mid-point review, final presentation
  • Your time cost: about an hour a week

What it deliberately leaves out

  • Anything outside the one scoped question — a second question is a second engagement
  • Implementation after the final week; we hand over, you run it
  • Analysis that needs paid data we cannot access, unless you hold the licence
  • Scopes we cannot do well in a semester — we say no, and say so early
One question, honestly scoped, beats five questions half-answered. The scoping call exists to find that one question.

The obvious question

"They're students — why should my board trust this?" Fair. Here's the answer.

Every deliverable passes an AI-assisted senior review before you see it — built by our branch, published in the open, checking evidence, logic, and clarity against professional consulting standards.

Its core rule: a finding must quote the document verbatim, or stay silent. No student consultancy we know of can show you its quality system. We just did.

The rule, as the reviewer applies it

A reviewer proposes a finding on your deck

It quotes your document verbatim The finding stands. It goes to the consultant, who decides the rewrite — the reviewer never edits.
It cannot produce the quote The finding is suppressed. An unquotable observation never reaches the consultant, and never reaches you.
And the honest limits, on the record: we are students, engagements take a semester, and we say no to scopes we can't do well. That "no" is part of the quality system too.
