# Directions 6–10: What the First Five Taught, and Five New Theses

Written 2026-07-09, after building V1–V5 and running fifteen Critic rounds across them. Per the build brief: what sang, what fell flat, what no direction has dared — then five invented theses.

## What sang (patterns to keep)

1. **Signature devices beat styling.** Every variant's best moments were *devices*, not aesthetics: V1's dimension lines that break for their text, V2's cargo codes and bill-of-lading card, V3's 180° numeral making a literal half-turn, V4's `180dc status` session whose output IS the proof row, V5's review-findings card. The Critic's "would I recognize it with the logo removed?" test is passed by devices, never by palettes.
2. **The engagement timeline is the strongest content block we have** — every variant's most praised section was its native rendering of scoping → proposal → project → presentation (dimensioned drawing, ports of call, proportional blocks, `[GATE]` session, stones on a dark band). Whatever wins, the timeline must be a first-class design object.
3. **Honesty as a designed register works.** Placeholders styled as revision marks / hazard plates / pending-warnings / seed packets were repeatedly called distinctive — *when* they were one consistent register per site and quieter than the real content. Two lessons inside the lesson: one register only, and scaffolding must never out-shout cargo.
4. **Copy voice carries mission fit.** "Our standards are not student-grade," "nothing on this sheet is invented," "the reviewer only teaches" — critics kept crediting the words with the seriousness the visuals sometimes lacked.

## What fell flat (failure modes the new five must not repeat)

1. **Generic scroll-reveal fades.** Killed by the Critic in every single variant. Rule now: one thesis-native motion per site, or none.
2. **The symmetric six-card service wall.** Three variants defaulted to a SaaS card grid for the six service areas and were called out every time. The six services need a *form* native to each thesis, not cards.
3. **Metaphor on the controls.** Flag-syntax CTAs, "dock with us" headings — whenever the conceit touched a button or heading the client must act on, the Critic called it a tax on the paying audience. Conceit in the chrome, plain language on the doors.
4. **Duplication across pages** reads as template filler; interior pages must compress or transform shared content, never paste it.
5. **Decoration that doesn't work.** Empty planes, unlabeled boxes, imperceptible textures — on sites claiming rigor, idle elements falsify the thesis. Every element needs a job.
6. **Color as a fake legend.** Cycling colors on labeled items implies meaning that isn't there; color must be either strictly semantic or strictly compositional.

## What no direction has dared yet

- Making the **deliverable itself** the design — we sell board-ready decks and show none of that form.
- **Evidence as layout** — quote-or-abstain is our deepest principle and it only ever appears as copy.
- **Type-only extremism** — every variant leans on grids, photos, or chrome; none dares to be only words.
- **Dutch vernacular beyond the De Stijl cliché** — polders, broadsheets, protest pamphlets: deeper national registers untouched.
- **Wit.** All five are serious. Students are allowed one grin.

## The five invented theses

**V6 — The Deck.** *Concept:* the website is a board-ready consulting deck — title slide, agenda, numbered slides with slide-frame chrome, an executive summary, an appendix; nav is a table of contents; the murder-board stamp of approval on the final slide. *Why it serves the mission:* clients hire us for exactly one artifact — this shows the product by being the product, and shows students what "board-ready" means before they ever join. (Never seen a consulting site do this literally.)

**V7 — Polder.** *Concept:* the Dutch polder as design system — horizontal strata (sky / dike / field / water) structure every page, a thin NAP datum line runs through the whole site annotating each section's "elevation," parcel-strip grids lay out content, and the quality system is drawn as the pump that keeps the polder dry. *Why it serves the mission:* the polder is the Netherlands' founding story of collective engineering against a shared threat — precisely the Delft×Rotterdam identity of engineering in service of community, without a drop of De Stijl cliché.

**V8 — De Courant.** *Concept:* a Dutch broadsheet front page — masthead, column rules, serif headlines with decks and bylines, captioned photos, recruitment as classified ads, and the branch's honesty rules printed as an actual editorial colophon ("what this paper will not print: invented statistics, fake testimonials…"). *Why it serves the mission:* the newspaper is the civic-trust register — it frames pro bono consulting as public-interest work and gives the quote-or-abstain principle its natural home: journalism's own rulebook.

**V9 — Pamflet.** *Concept (riskier than anything in the first five):* Dutch protest-pamphlet brutalism — no images, no cards, no chrome; only enormous typography (up to 18vw), one green, hard rules, and the site readable top-to-bottom as a manifesto you could wheat-paste to a wall; WCAG AA everywhere because making brutalism accessible is the flex. *Why it serves the mission:* students doing social-impact work is protest energy with professional discipline — this is the one direction that sounds like the students, not the consultancy.

**V10 — The Ledger.** *Concept (riskier, and never seen on any consulting site):* radical transparency as layout — the site is an open account book where every factual claim carries a numbered entry in a visible evidence margin (source, date, status), unverifiable lines are struck through as "cannot claim yet," and the footer is a public changelog. *Why it serves the mission:* it is quote-or-abstain turned into architecture — the only consultancy website anywhere that shows its receipts, aimed at the exact skeptical director the research says we must win.

*Build order: V6 → V10 → V9 → V7 → V8 (deck first because it's the strongest President-pitch; ledger and pamflet early while critique energy is high).*
