# Rubric — Consultant Track

Used by the comparison agent in every head-to-head match for the **Consultant** pool. This is a starting draft from the research — review and adjust the wording/weights before the cycle, then freeze it.

## How the judge uses it
For each match the agent receives two blinded **candidate packages (resume + motivation letter)** + this rubric + the calibrated anchor examples, scores each candidate on every dimension (1–4), then returns `winner`, `confidence`, and a 2–3 sentence rationale citing specific evidence. The **resume** is the main source for C1/C3/C4 (what they did); the **motivation letter** is the main source for **C5 (mission fit)** and a live writing sample for **C2 (communication)** — those signals barely appear on a CV, so weight the letter heavily there.

**Scale (1–4, no midpoint — forces a lean):**
- **4** — clear, specific evidence; quantified or outcome-linked
- **3** — adequate evidence; present but vague/generic
- **2** — thin; implied but not demonstrated
- **1** — none, or contradicting signal

## Dimensions

| # | Dimension | Weight | What "4" looks like on a resume |
|---|-----------|--------|----------------------------------|
| C1 | **Analytical / Problem-Solving** | **30%** | Decomposed a problem and drew an insight from data with a measurable outcome. *e.g. "Analyzed 3-year sales data to find SKU-level margin drag; proposed pricing change → +12% margin."* (2 = "assisted with data analysis"; 1 = none visible.) |
| C2 | **Communication / Structured Thinking** | **20%** | *(The motivation letter is itself a writing sample — judge its clarity and structure directly.)* Plus evidence of clear output under ambiguity — reports, decks, pitches, facilitation. *e.g. "Wrote and presented a 20-page report to a 15-person board; earned a follow-on commission."* |
| C3 | **Initiative / Ownership** | **20%** | Self-started or went beyond assigned scope unprompted. *e.g. "Spotted an onboarding gap → designed a buddy system, adopted org-wide."* (2 = passive committee participation.) |
| C4 | **Collaborative Impact** | **15%** | Contributed within a team toward a shared, named outcome (leading not required). (2 = generic "worked in a team"; 1 = solo activities only.) |
| C5 | **Motivation / Mission Fit** | **15%** | *(Mainly judged from the motivation letter.)* Genuine, sustained interest in social impact — a specific, personal reason for 180DC tied to real involvement (volunteer/NGO/social-enterprise), not a generic template. (2 = one-off event or copy-paste "I want to help"; 1 = no civic engagement and no specific reason.) |

**Total: 100%.**

## Hard instructions to the judge (anti-bias)
- **Do not** treat university ranking, brand-name employer, or GPA as evidence for any dimension. Evaluate behaviours and outcomes only.
- **Do not** penalize a non-business major, a non-target school, an unstated GPA, or limited professional experience — many strong 180DC applicants are early-stage students from any discipline.
- Reward **depth of impact over breadth of listing** (eight clubs ≠ quality).
- Require **outcome evidence, not keywords** ("Python", "strategic thinking" alone score nothing).
- Treat generic motivation language ("passionate about social impact") at face value only — look for specific, sustained, named evidence.

## Anchor examples (fill before freezing)
Provide 3–5 worked "A beats B because…" pairs, verdict-balanced (clear A-win, clear B-win, genuine tie), hand-labelled by chapter leaders. These calibrate the judge and stop it drifting toward its own priors. Lock them with the rubric.
