DELIVERABLE TEXT (one block per slide or section, with numbers):

**Slide 1. Title**
Client E should launch a paid membership scheme, starting with a single tier in the
autumn.

**Slide 2. The decision**
Client E asked whether a paid membership could diversify income away from project
grants. Our answer is yes, with a deliberately small start.

**Slide 3. Why income needs to diversify**
Project grants are 80% of Client E's income (2024 accounts) and each is single-year.
Two of five grants are not expected to renew, per the funder letters on file.

**Slide 4. Members are already there**
Client E has 1,400 people on its mailing list and a 32% event-attendance rate (box-office
data, 2024). This is an engaged base to convert.

**Slide 5. What comparable groups charge**
Three comparable local arts non-profits run membership at 40 to 60 pounds a year (their
public websites, 2025). Client E has no membership today.

**Slide 6. A single-tier start reduces risk**
Launching one tier at 45 pounds, rather than three tiers at once, keeps admin low and
lets Client E learn before it scales.

**Slide 7. What members get**
Priority booking, two members-only events a year and a discount with local partners.
These use capacity Client E already has.

**Slide 8. The numbers**
If 8% of the mailing list joins at 45 pounds, that is about 5,000 pounds in year one.
The 8% assumption is conservative against the 32% attendance rate and is the main thing
to test.

**Slide 9. Risks and what we are assuming**
The main risk is low conversion. We assume the mailing list is accurate and that
members-only events do not strain staff. A soft launch to the most engaged 200 contacts
tests both before the full launch.

**Slide 10. First steps**
The Client E coordinator owns the launch. Month one: soft launch to 200 contacts. Month
two: review conversion against the 8% target. Month three: full launch if the soft
launch clears 5%.
