### Case 01. Vague recommendation, unsourced 40% figure

Readiness R1, gold R1. Exact, and case 01 is one of the three that must match exactly.

Must-catch is the unsourced "Region B demand will grow 40% over the next three years".
It is found: blocking issue rule 4 quotes it verbatim, and finding 2 (Ungrounded headline
numbers) leads on it. It is **not** the top finding. The top finding is that the slide 8
option comparison is asserted rather than shown, which is gold issue 1 in substance

gap. Section E clause 2 asks only that the must-catch is found, and it is. Section F's
worked expectation for case 01 said the 40% figure would be the top finding, and it is
not, so prioritization is scored 2 rather than 3. This is the single most contestable
number on the sheet and section E clears either way, which is stated in section E below.

True positives: F1 unsupported comparison (gold 1), F2 unsourced Region B numbers (gold 1
must-catch), F4 next steps not actionable (gold 2), F5 late governing insight and
topic-label titles (gold 3).

False positive: the comment flagging the 620 tonnes figure on slide 4 as unsourced. The
gold names this exactly: "Flagging slide 3 or 4 facts as unsourced. Those are the client's

The review does hedge it ("It does not drive the decision") but it still put it on the
page.

Closest call in the set: F3 (capacity strain unaddressed). The gold lists as unacceptable

Restraint 2. Five findings is the ceiling of the deep budget on a deck the gold sizes at

## F. Disagreements with scoring-2026-07-03.md

Read once, after sections B to E above were written and fixed.

**Where I agree.**

- The five-case verdict of that round, NOT GREEN, was right on the evidence in front of it.
  The case 05 run it scored (`case-05-review-v2-live.json`) returned R2 with three findings
  all graded major and two delivery-critical. That fails section E clause 1 on readiness and
  clause 4 on restraint. It was correct to call it, and correct to call it on the one case
  the whole harness exists to protect.
- Its diagnosis, that the three case 05 observations were real but the severity was inflated
  and the demotion to R2 was the manufactured over-escalation the case is built to catch, is
  the right reading. The r2 run confirms it: the same reviewer, given a severity-calibration
  fix, kept a version of one of those observations, graded it minor and held R3.
- Its refusal to let a strong scorecard justify R2 matches rubric section B, which says in
  as many words that a strong scorecard with no blocking issue and no decision-changing gap
  is R3.

**Where I disagree.**

- **It scored case 05's fix as complete without a written re-score.** That is the gap this
  sheet exists to close, and it is a process failure rather than a judgment failure. The
  fixed run is dated 2026-07-03 17:31, ten minutes after scoring-2026-07-03.md's own
  timestamp. A green claim resting on a file written before the artifact it blesses is not
  a green claim.
- **It is more forgiving of the comment layer than I am.** It treats the comment stream as
  lighter-weight and largely outside the precision count. I count comments as flagged items,
  because a comment is still something the review puts in front of a team, and on that count
  all four of the set's false positives sit in the comments. That does not change the section
  E verdict, since precision clears either way, but it changes where the next prompt fix
  should point.
- **On case 02 I am harder than that round was.** I score the unsourced-closure-evidence
  finding a false positive by weight, because escalating it to a rule 2 blocking issue puts
  the reviewer's rule 2 on the scope-switched recommendation instead of on the client's
  unanswered question, which is where the gold's rule 2 sits. The readiness level is right
  for a partly wrong reason, and that is worth logging rather than passing over.
- **On case 01 I am harder on prioritization.** Section F names the 40% figure as the
  expected top finding for this case, and in this run it is ranked second behind the
  comparison gap. I score prioritization 2. That does not change the verdict, as section E
  clause 3 above sets out.

**One thing neither round can settle.** Both scorings run against the same five synthetic

## G. On the case 05 adjudication note

The gold for case 05 carries an adjudication note dated 2026-07-03, added after the live run
and after the fresh-context scoring of it, and kept visibly separate from the pre-registered
gold above it.

**I accept the adjudication. I do not think the gold is wrong.**

Reasons, in the order that matters.

1. **It does not move the answer.** Readiness stays R3, which is what the pre-registered
   gold said before any run existed. The note changes nothing that the tool is scored
   against on the criterion that decides this case.
2. **It concedes ground to the tool rather than taking it.** The note accepts that all three
   of the live run's observations are legitimate, including one the pre-registered gold had
   not thought of (the soft-launch gate at 5% sitting below the 8% the revenue rests on, and
   running on the most engaged 200 contacts). That is a real gap in the original answer key
   and the note says so. A gold rewritten to protect itself would have called those three
   points false positives. This one calls them fair minors and still fails the run, on
   severity.
3. **The severity call is right on the rubric as written, not on the note's say-so.** Rubric
   section B defines major as a finding the team must close before submission because
   closing it changes the recommendation or its defensibility. None of the three changes the
   recommendation. The deck already flags 8% as the thing to test and already builds a soft
   launch to de-risk it. So minor is the correct grade under a rule that predates the run,
   and the note is applying the rubric rather than inventing a standard.
4. **The structural hygiene is right.** Appending a dated note below the original rather
   than editing the answer key means a later reader can see exactly what was known before
   the run and what was learned after. That is the only version of post-hoc gold refinement
   that does not quietly become overfitting.

**The reservation.** A gold file amended after seeing the tool's output is a real
methodological cost even when handled this well, and the fact that the amendment sharpened
the case in the tool's disfavour is what makes it acceptable here rather than a general
licence. The note also asserts what the fix should be ("a severity-calibration change to the
prompt and rubric"), which is a builder's judgment sitting inside an answer key. It belongs
in the decision log, not in the gold. That is a placement quibble, not a reason to reject it.

I would not accept a future amendment to any gold in this set that loosened a constraint,
added a permitted finding after the tool produced it, or moved a readiness level. This one
does none of those.

## H. Limits. What this scoring does not establish

- **It does not establish that the reviewer works on real decks.** All five cases are
  synthetic, short (nine or ten slides of plain text) and each was built to contain one
  named failure. Real deliverables arrive long, messy, multi-failure and sometimes with no
  clean failure at all. Nothing here predicts that.
- **It does not establish independence from the case author.** The decks, the golds, the
  rubric and the prompt come from the same hand. A tool scoring well against an answer key
  written by its own designer is measuring internal consistency. The correct next evidence
  is the blind human labeling on real decks, not more synthetic cases.
- **It does not establish stability.** Each score is one sample of one run. Two of the five
  outputs are second runs (`-r2`), which tells you the first attempt was not always right.
  Nothing here measures how often the reviewer lands R0 on case 04, or how wide the spread
  is across ten runs of the same input. Green on a single sample is not green on a
  distribution.
- **It does not establish that expert consultants agree with the golds.** Section E gates
  expert time. It does not substitute for it. If an expert reads case 02 and says the
  mentoring analysis is genuinely weak, the gold moves and case 02's precision moves with it.
- **It does not re-derive the prompt-side cause of anything.** This sheet scores outputs. It
  does not diff the prompt between the pre-fix and post-fix case 05 runs, so it cannot say
  what the fix was or whether the fix was narrow enough to avoid the overfitting that harness
  section E warns about. Two of the five outputs being second runs is exactly the condition
  under which that warning applies.
- **It does not audit the validator.** `check-review-v2.js` had uncommitted local
  modifications at the time of this run, so the "five of five VALID" line above is a result
  from the working-tree validator and not necessarily from the committed one. The validator
  also passed an out-of-schema field on case 05 without comment, so its field checking is
  known to be permissive.
- **It scores one scorer.** Rubric section F sets the calibration bar as two people landing
  on the same readiness level and within one point on eight of ten dimensions. This sheet is
  one person. The agreement with scoring-2026-07-03.md on the pre-fix case 05 is suggestive
  but it is not that test, because that round scored a different artifact.
