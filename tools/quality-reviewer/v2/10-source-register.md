# 10. Source register

Every source that justifies a v2 design decision, with the exact claim used, why it is
relevant, its limitation and the rubric dimensions it supports. A source does not
belong here because it is impressive. It belongs here because a design decision rests
on it. If research access is available, verify each source. Where a source cannot be
verified from the material to hand, it is marked so and verification is a required next
action.

Voice rules apply. No Oxford commas. No em or en dashes.

Status key: Verified (checked against the primary source or an authoritative summary),
Practitioner standard (working MBB convention, not a single paper), Unverified (cited
in prior research, not re-checked here), Withdrawn (must not be relied on).

---

## Sources in use

### S1. Minto Pyramid Principle
- **Citation.** Barbara Minto, The Pyramid Principle. MBB working standard since the
  1960s.
- **Claim used.** The answer comes first, every element supports it, and a reader of
  the titles alone can reconstruct the argument. Action titles are complete-sentence
  assertions.
- **Relevance.** The backbone of dimensions 3 and 9 and the horizontal-flow test.
- **Limitation.** A convention, not an empirical study. Do not present it as evidence
  of outcomes, only as the craft standard.
- **Dimensions.** 3, 9.
- **Status.** Practitioner standard.

### S2. SCR narrative (Situation, Complication, Resolution)
- **Citation.** McKinsey storytelling convention, paired with S1.
- **Claim used.** A deck reads as an argument when it moves from a neutral situation to
  the complication that forces a decision to the resolution.
- **Relevance.** Supports the storyline flow test in dimension 3.
- **Limitation.** Convention, not a study. Not every artifact type needs a full SCR arc.
- **Dimensions.** 3.
- **Status.** Practitioner standard.

### S3. ERIP model of consulting service quality
- **Citation.** Veres and Varga-Toldi (2020), Journal of Business and Industrial
  Marketing, DOI 10.1108/JBIM-05-2020-0226. Grounded in 22 expert interviews.
- **Claim used.** Consulting quality has four dimensions: Expertise, Relations,
  Involvement, Performance. A deliverable is judged by whether the client can act on it
  (Performance) and whether it fits the client (Involvement).
- **Relevance.** Grounds dimensions 1, 4, 7. Reinforces that a deliverable alone is
  necessary but not sufficient.
- **Limitation.** Derived from financial-sector consulting. Generalizable but not
  student-specific.
- **Dimensions.** 1, 4, 7.
- **Status.** Verified.

### S4. Dell'Acqua et al, the jagged frontier
- **Citation.** Dell'Acqua et al, Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier, Harvard
  Business School working paper 24-013 (2023). Pre-registered field experiment with 758
  BCG consultants. Journal version: Organization Science (2026), DOI
  10.1287/orsc.2025.21838. Cite the working paper for the 2023 date or the journal for
  the published version, never a blend.
- **Claim used.** Inside the frontier, consultants using AI completed 12.2 percent more
  tasks, 25.1 percent faster, with quality more than 40 percent higher, and the bottom
  half of performers gained most (about 43 percent). On the task outside the frontier,
  AI-assisted consultants were 19 percentage points less likely to produce a correct
  solution.
- **Relevance.** The strategic rationale for the whole tool and the equity case (helps
  weaker teams most). The frontier caveat justifies the abstention and human-sign-off
  rules.
- **Limitation.** Professional BCG consultants, not students. Use as directional
  evidence, not a promised number.
- **Dimensions.** Meta (rationale), and the safety posture behind dimension 10.
- **Status.** Verified (figures re-checked against the working paper on 2026-07-02).

### S5. Ericsson deliberate practice
- **Citation.** Ericsson, deliberate practice literature (for example 2008, Academic
  Emergency Medicine).
- **Claim used.** Expert performance needs immediate, specific, task-focused feedback
  with a chance to attempt again. Years of experience alone correlate weakly with
  performance.
- **Relevance.** Grounds the learning loop: a reflect question before the fix, and the
  separated learning block. Feedback teaches only when the consultant attempts first.
- **Limitation.** General expertise research, not consulting-specific.
- **Dimensions.** The learning block and the coaching gate, not a single dimension.
- **Status.** Verified.

### S6. Student consulting failure modes (guest essay on Tom Spencer's blog)
- **Citation.** Sam Smith (2018), "Student Consulting. Does it deliver?", guest post on
  spencertom.com, 24 June 2018,
  https://www.spencertom.com/2018/06/24/student-consulting-does-it-deliver/
- **Claim used.** Student consulting output is often "more akin to a research report
  than problem analysis or strategy". Root causes: thin domain expertise, weak
  supervision, academic mental model.
- **Relevance.** Grounds dimension 5 (description versus analysis) and the failure
  taxonomy behind the diagnostic priority order.
- **Limitation.** A practitioner essay, not a study. Directional. The same site
  published a rebuttal ("Student consulting. It does more than deliver", August 2018),
  so cite it as one side of a debate, not a settled view. Previously misattributed to
  Tom Spencer himself.
- **Dimensions.** 2, 5.
- **Status.** Verified (URL and verbatim quote confirmed, author corrected to Sam Smith
  on 2026-07-03).

### S7. Shi and Omachonu, consulting quality gaps
- **Citation.** Shi and Omachonu (2016), Universal Journal of Management 4(7), 375-385.
- **Claim used.** Four gaps: needs versus heard, stated versus unstated objectives,
  planned versus delivered, delivered versus perceived value. That these gaps are worse
  in student contexts is our inference, not the paper's finding.
- **Relevance.** Grounds dimension 2 (framing) and dimension 1 (perceived usefulness).
- **Limitation.** Academic model, general consulting. The publisher (HRPub) is of
  contested quality, so present this as a conceptual framework, not as evidence.
- **Dimensions.** 1, 2.
- **Status.** Verified (paper exists as cited, author name corrected 2026-07-02).

### S8. Cognitive apprenticeship applied to LLMs
- **Citation.** arXiv 2601.19053 (January 2026).
- **Claim used.** Cognitively informed prompts (modeling, coaching, scaffolding,
  articulation, reflection, exploration) elicit deeper reasoning and more reflective
  feedback than baseline prompting.
- **Relevance.** Design basis for the reflect-before-fix gate and the coaching mode.
- **Limitation.** Recent, single study, within-subjects, and the domain is
  data-visualization design mentoring, not consulting. The paper itself notes baseline
  prompting was sometimes preferred. Treat as supporting, not decisive.
- **Dimensions.** The coaching gate.
- **Status.** Verified (existence and scope re-checked against the arXiv record on
  2026-07-02).

### S9. CMCE consultant value-add research
- **Citation.** Centre for Management Consulting Excellence (January 2021), Consultant
  Value Add: Maximising Value from your Management Consultant. Survey ran late 2019,
  161 respondents (clients, consulting firm representatives and independent
  consultants) plus follow-up interviews.
  https://www.cmce.org.uk/projects/consultant-value-add-maximising-value-your-management-consultant
- **Claim used.** 57 percent of client respondents included coaching client staff to
  perform project activities in their top three ways consultants deliver value.
- **Relevance.** Supports the teaching mandate and dimension 1 (usefulness includes
  building client capacity).
- **Limitation.** Practitioner survey, n=161 across mixed respondent types, and the
  client-only subsample behind the 57 percent is not stated. The figure is a top-three
  ranking, not "the best way" as a single choice.
- **Dimensions.** 1.
- **Status.** Verified (report, verbatim claim and sample checked on 2026-07-03).

### S10. FeedbackWriter RCT
- **Citation.** arXiv 2602.16820, N = 354.
- **Claim used.** AI-assisted feedback produced significantly higher-quality revisions,
  Cohen's d about 0.50. Post-test learning-outcome scores showed no significant
  difference.
- **Relevance.** Evidence that AI-assisted feedback improves the work, with an honest
  null on durable learning that the expert pack should disclose.
- **Limitation.** Not consulting, and the AI suggestions went to teaching assistants
  who delivered the feedback, not directly to students. Transfer to a direct AI
  reviewer is our extrapolation.
- **Dimensions.** Meta (learning claims).
- **Status.** Verified (with the null caveat).

## Dropped or cautioned sources

### D1. arXiv 2605.17554, five-criterion consulting rubric
- **Status.** Withdrawn on 2026-05-30.
- **Action.** Do not cite as an authority. The five criteria it proposed (data
  integrity, analytical rigor, relevance and focus, execution precision, format and
  deliverability) are defensible on their own and overlap our dimensions, but the
  design must rest on S1 to S10, not on this paper. v1's SYSTEM-PROMPT references it
  implicitly and should be scrubbed if v1 is ever revised.
