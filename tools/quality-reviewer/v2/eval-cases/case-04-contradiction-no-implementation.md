# Case 04. Internal contradiction and overconfident conclusion

Synthetic and fully anonymized. Built to test whether the reviewer catches an internal
contradiction that the recommendation walks into (the R0 blocking rule), plus
overconfidence and a missing implementation path.

- **Client (anonymized):** Client D, a social enterprise selling refurbished bicycles
  and training young people in repair skills.
- **Artifact type:** final recommendation deck.
- **Client question:** How can Client D become financially self-sustaining within two
  years.
- **Draft maturity:** near final, 9 slides.

---

**Slide 1. Title**
Client D path to self-sustainability. A two-year plan.

**Slide 2. Situation**
Client D covers 60% of its costs from bicycle sales and relies on grants for the rest.
Grant income is expected to fall.

**Slide 3. Financial position**
Client D has no cash reserves and cannot take on new fixed costs without new income
first. The board has been clear that hiring is not possible until revenue grows.

**Slide 4. The market**
Demand for refurbished bikes is strong and growing in the city. Client D turns away
repair work it cannot staff.

**Slide 5. The opportunity**
A dedicated retail shopfront would lift sales and visibility. Comparable social
enterprises have doubled revenue after opening a shop.

**Slide 6. Our recommendation**
Open a retail shopfront within six months and hire three paid repair staff to meet
demand. This will make Client D self-sustaining.

**Slide 7. Financial impact**
Revenue will double in year one. The shop pays for itself by month nine.

**Slide 8. Why this works**
The plan is low-risk. Demand is proven and the model is tested by peers.

**Slide 9. Next steps**
Sign a lease. Recruit staff. Launch.
