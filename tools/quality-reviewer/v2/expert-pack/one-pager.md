# AI Quality Reviewer. One page for the expert reviewer

Thank you for looking at this. It should take 30 to 60 minutes. Voice note: this
document avoids Oxford commas and dashes on purpose, it is a house style, not a typo.

## What it is

An AI reviewer for student consulting deliverables at 180 Degrees Consulting
Delft-Rotterdam, a pro-bono student consultancy. Student teams advise non-profits. There
is usually no senior practitioner reviewing the work before it reaches the client. This
tool is that missing senior read. It finds the highest-risk weaknesses before client
submission and coaches the team on the reasoning behind each fix.

## What we want from you

One judgment: does it find the issues that matter and give feedback that would improve
the deliverable's usefulness to the client. Not whether it sounds polished. Whether you
would agree with its priorities.

## What it does and does not do

It identifies the top 3 to 5 risks, explains why each matters for the client's decision,
quotes the exact text it is critiquing, separates delivery-critical issues from polish
and from a learning note, and coaches without rewriting the deck. It never approves a
deliverable, never rewrites whole decks, never invents sources or client facts, and
never judges or ranks individual people. A human project lead signs off. The AI advises.

## How readiness works (the one design choice to know)

Readiness is not an average of scores. It is set by blocking issues. A deck with clean
tone and tidy slides is still Not ready if its recommendation contradicts its own
evidence or rests on an unsourced number. Four levels: Not ready for client review,
Needs substantial revision, Needs targeted revision, Nearly ready with minor edits.

## The ten-dimension rubric (in brief)

1. Client decision usefulness. 2. Problem framing. 3. Storyline and pyramid logic.
4. Evidence quality. 5. Analysis and insight. 6. Recommendation specificity.
7. Feasibility and implementation. 8. Risks, assumptions and uncertainty.
9. Slide-level communication. 10. Professionalism, tone and confidentiality.

Each is scored through yes or no sub-checks, not a single impression. Scores are
diagnostic. Readiness comes from the blocking issues.

## The three samples

You are looking at three deliberately different decks so you can judge whether the tool
discriminates: a broken deck, a structurally weak deck and a strong deck. On the strong
one, the right answer is a short and generous review. We want to know if it manages that
or invents problems to look busy.

Each review you see is a live output, generated blind by the tool from the deck and the
filled input, not written by hand. The strong sample is the run after a
severity-calibration fix: an earlier version over-escalated that deck, our eval caught it
and the fix corrected it to a short generous review. We are showing you the corrected run
and we are happy to show you the earlier miss if that helps you judge the loop.

## Honest limits

The tool reviews only the text it is given, it does not verify external facts it cannot
see, and it flags rather than adjudicates confidentiality. In one study of AI-assisted
instructor feedback (AI suggestions delivered through teaching assistants, not directly
to students), revisions improved but durable learning scores did not move, so we treat
the learning claim as a hypothesis we are testing, not a promise. All samples here are synthetic and
anonymized. No real client material is used.
