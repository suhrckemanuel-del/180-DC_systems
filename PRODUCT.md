# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences share every page, and they want opposite things.

**Organisations considering bringing us a challenge.** Non-profits, social
enterprises and socially minded companies, mostly small. The person who makes
first contact is not yet a known persona — the branch has too few inquiries to
see a pattern — so the intake is designed for the widest case: someone who may
be a founder wearing every hat, a programme manager with a specific problem, or
a board member who does not own the decision. Assume they are busy, assume they
have not framed the problem yet, and assume they are quietly unsure whether they
qualify or can afford it.

**Students considering applying.** They are choosing between this and a dozen
other campus commitments, and they are here for learning and career value.

The site also serves a third, non-obvious reader: the branch board itself, which
uses the variants gallery to pick a direction.

## Product Purpose

Get an organisation to make contact, and get good students to apply, without
overstating what a student-run branch is. Success is a first email that would not
otherwise have been sent.

The whole engagement runs on two consulting cycles a year (October–January,
March–June), so the site is not a continuous funnel; it is a door that has to be
obviously open when someone happens to walk past.

## Brand Personality

**Rigorous, credible, precise.** The work is done by students, and the site
never hides that, so credibility cannot be borrowed from seniority. It has to be
demonstrated: named cycles, a proposal signed before work begins, real
photographs with real credits, placeholders where content does not exist yet.

The voice is plain and specific. It does not sell, it states. Where something is
not built, it says so.

## Anti-references

- **A student club sign-up sheet.** Nothing that reads as extracurricular,
  amateur, or low-stakes. This is a real engagement with a real deliverable.
- **A charity fundraising appeal.** No emotive imagery, no urgency language, no
  donation grammar. The visitor *is* the charity; we are the supplier.
- **Big-consultancy gravitas** is a softer no: formality and jargon would
  intimidate a four-person organisation.
- **Startup SaaS signup.** No "Get started free", no gradient buttons, no
  progress-bar onboarding.
- **Invented content of any kind.** No stock faces as our team, no AI-generated
  imagery, no illustrative case studies, no placeholder impact numbers. Reserved
  space stays visibly reserved.
- Not heavy on saturated colour or dark bands. 180DC's own site is
  white-dominant with green as accent, and the client asked for more white.

## Design Principles

1. **Honesty is the differentiator.** A visible placeholder beats a plausible
   fabrication. The site's credibility comes from the things it refuses to claim,
   and that refusal is stated out loud rather than hidden.

2. **The ask is small; the standards are shown.** Lowering the barrier to contact
   must not lower the perceived seriousness of the work. Rigour is carried by
   what surrounds the ask — what happens next, what gets signed, when the cycles
   run — never by making the visitor prove themselves first.

3. **Measure, do not eyeball.** Legibility, contrast and weight are audited
   against real rendered pixels at real viewport sizes, on a real GPU. A number
   that was never checked against what ships is worse than no number.

4. **The page works before JavaScript.** Every enhancement is progressive; the
   content, the navigation and the contact route are all real HTML first.

5. **Terminology is brand-controlled, not editorial.** The global org publishes a
   yes/no table and it wins over house preference.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA is the floor and is enforced by the audit suites, not left to
judgement: contrast is sampled from real pixels behind real copy at both 1440px
and 390px, by worst-tile as well as by mean. `prefers-reduced-motion` is
respected and re-evaluated on change. Focus rings are verified under real Tab
traversal, heading order is checked on every page, and hit targets are measured.

Visitors may be reading in a second language: sentences stay short and concrete,
and no meaning is carried by colour alone.
