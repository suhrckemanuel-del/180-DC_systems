# Quality and brand review notes

## Method applied

The review used Reviewer V2 as a controlled thinking method, not as an autonomous scorer or approver. Each candidate was tested for the client decision, claim, evidence, comparator, implication, action, owner or timing and uncertainty. Visual polish did not compensate for a missing decision or unsafe content.

Reviewer V2 itself carries an important trust boundary. Its findings are the useful product. Its readiness verdict is not a publication decision. This review therefore uses slide-level evidence and human judgement. It does not quote a Reviewer recall or coverage claim.

## Portfolio-level findings

1. **The strongest portfolio story is a sequence, not one decorative page.** SCEF and OAF work because the selected exhibits show different reasoning jobs. GoodHout earns the third slot through commercial breadth and strong visual communication, but it needs restrained captions because its evidence chain is weaker.
2. **Original slides create a higher consent burden.** Every featured case contains a client name, logo or identifying context. The private preview can show them for board review. Production cannot.
3. **No outcome is evidenced.** The source material shows analyses, frameworks, recommendations and intended implementation. It does not establish client adoption, revenue growth, fundraising gains, impact or endorsement.
4. **The old 18-slide gallery is not a sufficient selection authority.** It includes useful assets, but it also includes title or divider pages, report pages that are illegible in a modal and action plans that describe project delivery rather than client implementation.
5. **The wider archive changed the shortlist.** SCEF displaces BCI as the strongest operating-strategy case. GoodHout remains useful after the broader scan because it adds commercial execution and a coherent visual system. Circular IQ is a reserve because only two clean original slides survive the current risk screen.

## Highest-value review findings

- **OAF:** the matrix is decision-rich, but equal weighting and missing stakeholder input can change the rank. Show the limitations page beside the matrix. Verify the linked spreadsheet before any public claim about ranking.
- **SCEF:** the lifecycle from framework design to evaluation and renewal is strong. Keep captions in the present or proposed tense until implementation is evidenced. Inspect embedded forms for hidden client fields.
- **GoodHout:** the recommendation is clear, but the link from a small qualitative survey to the final priority is not fully visible. Do not describe it as validated customer evidence. Treat the SEO framework as a method and the final slide as advice.
- **Stahili:** matrices communicate the choice well, but the evaluation method and stakeholder classification are under-sourced. The implementation slide lacks owner, timing and success signals.
- **Circular IQ:** slides 30 and 32 are persuasive strategy options, not evidence of a decision taken. Other candidate slides either expose drafting notes or create third-party comparison risk.

## Preview-only protection and verification

- Store original source files only in the existing private archive.
- Keep preview image derivatives under a gitignored private path, separate from `website/variants/dist` and the V15 production asset path.
- The production builder should fail or omit the preview asset directory unless a completed case-specific approval record is present.
- Verify every selected asset at desktop width and at a narrow mobile viewport. Confirm text remains inspectable through zoom or full-screen viewing.
- Verify keyboard open, close, previous and next controls, focus return, no-JavaScript links, alt text, captions, asset loading and broken-link behaviour.
- Re-run the name, logo, personal-data, live-link, commercial-sensitivity and third-party-claim audit on the exact exported images, not only on extracted text.
- Do not deploy or push source assets until the lead has approved the preview and each public case has a consent record.
