# V15 "What a deliverable looks like" section

This is the content and publishing specification for the section shown on the V15 client page. It is deliberately a **case-study gallery**, not a download library. It turns the five selected source deliverables into short proof points while keeping original client material private.

## The page section

Keep the screenshot's visual structure: eyebrow, heading, concise explanation, then a three-card grid. Replace the current reserved placeholders only when a card has a completed `PUBLIC-OK` record.

### Final section copy

**Eyebrow**
OUR WORK

**Heading**
What a deliverable looks like

**Introduction**
Every engagement ends in a decision-ready recommendation: grounded in evidence, clear about trade-offs and built for action. The case studies below show the work itself, not a claim of outcomes we cannot verify.

**Small trust note**
Selected examples are anonymised or published with client approval. We never publish a source deck or confidential client information.

## Card anatomy

Each card uses exactly the visual rhythm in the reference image.

| Element | Purpose | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Service tag | States the type of work | One of the branch service areas, not a client name |
| Decision-led title | Makes the case memorable | A client question or decision, not `Deliverable 3` |
| One-sentence summary | Explains why the work mattered | No outcome claim unless approved |
| Situation | The decision context | 16-24 words, anonymised |
| Approach | How the team got there | Name a method, not a vague activity |
| What we delivered | The immediate output | Replaces `Outcome` until an outcome is verified and approved |
| View case study | Opens the short detail page | Never download the source deck |

Do not use client logos, real screenshots, client names or performance figures on the card by default.

## The case-study page behind each card

Each `View case study` link should open a short 5-7 section narrative, not a slide gallery:

1. The decision question and one-sentence answer
2. Engagement scope and service area
3. The two or three evidence streams used
4. Up to three approved insights
5. Approved recommendations or actions
6. One reconstructed exhibit
7. A client-enquiry call to action

The exact source-to-site path is:

`original deliverable -> private source pack -> approved claim and exhibit register -> Reviewer pass -> lead triage -> PUBLIC-OK -> card and case-study page`

## What must be approved for each card

- the card title and service tag;
- its three rows of copy;
- its one reconstructed exhibit;
- whether the client is named or anonymised; and
- every outcome claim, if any.

Until those five items are cleared, leave the V15 placeholder in place. This keeps the site honest while the work is being made portfolio-ready.
