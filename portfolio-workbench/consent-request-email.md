# Consent request — ready to send

One email per client. Send from a named Consulting Director, not a shared inbox: this is
a favour being asked of a person who worked with us, and it should read that way.

Attach the exact pages you want to publish, as a PDF. Never ask for blanket permission
to publish "the deliverable". Ask about the specific pages, because that is what makes a
yes meaningful and what makes a no cheap for them to give.

---

## The email

> **Subject:** Permission to show two pages of our work together
>
> Hi [name],
>
> We are building a portfolio page for the 180 Degrees Consulting Delft–Rotterdam branch
> so prospective clients can see the kind of work we do before they get in touch.
>
> We would like to include a small part of the work we did with you. Attached are the
> exact pages we would publish, [N] in total. Nothing else from the project would appear.
>
> To be clear about what we are asking:
>
> - These are the original pages, so [organisation] would be named and your logo is
>   visible on them.
> - We would say what the question was and what we recommended. We would not claim any
>   result or outcome on your behalf.
> - No figures, no internal data, no named individuals appear on these pages.
> - It would sit at [URL].
>
> If any of that is not comfortable, we have easy alternatives and no hard feelings:
>
> 1. **Publish anonymously.** Same pages, your name and logo removed, described only as
>    "an organisation working in [sector]".
> 2. **Publish a rebuilt version.** We redraw the page in our own template with the
>    specifics taken out, so it shows the method and not your project.
> 3. **Publish nothing.** Just say no and that is the end of it.
>
> If you are happy with the original as attached, a reply saying so is enough. If you
> would like a change first, tell us what to take out and we will send it back.
>
> Thanks either way, and for the project.
>
> [name]
> Consulting Director, 180 Degrees Consulting Delft–Rotterdam

---

## Before you send

- [ ] Attach the exact pages, as a PDF. Not the deck.
- [ ] Remove any page the review held. Do not ask about a page you already know is a
      problem, because a yes to it is worse than a no.
- [ ] Confirm the recipient still works there and is senior enough to give permission.
      A programme manager saying yes is not the organisation saying yes.
- [ ] Check the pages one last time for a third party, a figure or an individual.

## When a reply comes back

Record it. A yes in someone's inbox is not a record the branch can rely on after the
sender graduates. Fill in `PUBLIC-OK-template.md` per client with who agreed, their role,
the date, the exact pages and the scope, and keep the reply itself alongside it.

A yes covers the pages that were attached. It does not extend to other pages later.
