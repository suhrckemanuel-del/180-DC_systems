# Portfolio case-study selection rubric

Use this rubric after an initial machine triage. The triage identifies candidates. It does not choose winners.

## Non-negotiable publication-safety gates

All five answers must be **yes** before a candidate can be selected for public adaptation.

1. **Permission:** A current branch lead can confirm that the client has agreed to a public case study, or that a fully anonymised account is permitted.
2. **Re-identification:** The story, sector, geography, unusual facts, screenshots and numbers cannot identify the client unless they have approved identification.
3. **Personal data:** Names, email addresses, phone numbers, consultant biographies, signatures and meeting details are removed unless the people have consented.
4. **Commercial and operational sensitivity:** Pricing, funder/investor targets, non-public strategy, internal weaknesses, raw research, and forward-looking financials are removed or replaced with approved abstractions.
5. **Truth:** Every public claim, outcome and number is traceable to the original work or to a client-approved update. If it cannot be verified, it does not appear.

Any “no” means **hold**. It is not a redaction task to be solved silently.

## Selection score: 100 points

Score each criterion from 0 to 5, then multiply by its weight.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 5 means |
|---|---:|---|
| Client decision value | 20 | The work clearly helped answer a consequential client decision or unlock a next action. |
| Story completeness | 15 | Challenge, approach, insight, recommendation and next step form one understandable arc. |
| Evidence and reasoning | 15 | Claims have credible support, limits are surfaced and recommendations follow from the analysis. |
| Actionability | 15 | The client can see what to do next, who acts, and how progress could be tracked. |
| Exhibit quality | 15 | At least three distinct, legible visual exhibits can be rebuilt without exposing sensitive material. |
| Portfolio contribution | 10 | It adds a service area, sector, or type of decision not already represented by selected cases. |
| Adaptation efficiency | 5 | The public version can be made strong without inventing outcomes or rebuilding the entire source artifact. |
| Publication readiness | 5 | Consent and safety are simple to establish, even though the gates still decide eligibility. |

### Thresholds

- **78–100:** selected, subject to signed-off safety gates.
- **65–77:** reserve candidate. Select only if it fills an intentional portfolio gap.
- **Below 65:** do not adapt for the initial launch.

## Evaluation evidence to record

For every score, record:

- the source file and relevant slide/page number;
- one short verbatim quote or a precise exhibit description;
- why it earns the score; and
- any limitation or sensitivity risk.

No score can be based on an inferred client outcome.

## Case-study rebuild brief

Each selected case becomes a new 5–7 screen public story, not a source-deck excerpt:

1. **The question** — one sentence describing the client decision, with the client named only if approved.
2. **The engagement** — service area, time frame, team and the scope of work.
3. **What we examined** — the 2–3 methods or evidence streams that mattered.
4. **What we found** — up to three decision-relevant insights, carefully worded and traceable.
5. **What changed** — approved recommendations or actions. Outcomes only when verified and approved.
6. **A selected exhibit** — a public-safe reconstruction, not a screenshot by default.
7. **Call to action** — invite a prospective client to bring a comparable question.

## Reviewer gate for the public version

Before website implementation, the public case study receives a scoped Quality Reviewer pass:

- Quote-or-abstain: every substantive claim is anchored to the approved source pack.
- Decision-led: title and lead sentence name the question and answer, not the activity.
- Evidence and logic: findings distinguish facts, assumptions and recommendations.
- Reader test: a prospective client can understand the relevance without the original deck.
- Noise budget: no more than five highest-risk improvements per pass.
- Human control: a named lead decides which findings to keep and signs the `PUBLIC-OK.md` record.
